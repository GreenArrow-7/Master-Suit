## 3. Recovery-case handling

`OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED → RESOLUTION_PROPOSED → RESOLVED`, with `assigneeId`. Acknowledging records a note and moves no money (asserted: outcome stays null). Recording a recovery requires a positive amount, a reference and a provider/bank transaction id or a document (DB check `recovered_evidence_check`). A write-off or an accepted adjustment is proposed by one person and approved by a different one (`collections:APPROVE`; DB check `two_people_check`; `writeoff_approval_check`). A reversal case cannot be closed as an adjustment. Nothing debits anyone; paid commissions and paid payouts are re-read after every path and are unchanged.

### 5.1 The ordering guarantee — reversal racing payment, stated precisely

Both `decideReceipt` (verifying a reversal) and `decidePayout(PAID)` begin by `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the **booking row(s)** — the payout path in ascending booking-id order for every booking behind its lines. PostgreSQL serialises them on that lock; each holds it to commit. So exactly one of two orders happens:

- **Reversal first.** Its transaction verifies the reversal, recomputes coverage, regresses the unpaid commission to `CONFIRMED`, detaches it, reopens the run to `DRAFT`, commits. The payment transaction then acquires the lock, re-reads the run, finds the commission gone / coverage short, and is **refused** (`not_covered:short`). Nothing was paid.
- **Payment first.** Its transaction re-checks coverage (still covered), marks the run `PAID` and the commissions `PAID`, commits. The reversal transaction then acquires the lock, finds `PAID` commission, and opens a **recovery case**. The payment stands.

What this guarantees: the database state is one of those two, never a payout marked paid on money the ledger no longer shows as verified without a case saying so. **What it does not guarantee, said plainly:** `PAID` is a row in this database. **No bank or payment-provider integration exists or was tested.** The tests prove the ledger's ordering; they do not prove — and must not be read to imply — that an external transfer initiated before the reversal can be stopped or undone. Reversing money that has left the agency's account is what the recovery case is for.
